Showroom — Bay 01 / 03 · In-house, Matrimony.com

WedAssist

An in-house product that brings wedding services, selection and payment into one connected journey, continued by a Relationship Manager.

Role
Product Designer
Scope
Selection to payment
Status
Original in-house design

A service with a human continuation

WedAssist pairs online service selection and payment with a dedicated Relationship Manager. The challenge: choosing relevant services and understanding cost and next steps before paying.

Smaller decisions, kept in context

A side drawer preserves the package while categories are chosen, and submission unlocks once the requirement is met. OTP errors offer recovery, and wedding details come before checkout.

Clear outcomes at commitment

Payment review and explicit success and failure states lead into Relationship Manager follow-up. I owned the UX/UI screens and connected interactions, working with product, design leadership and engineering.

Showroom — Bay 02 / 03 · Independent concept

ProductPulse AI

A connected workspace that helps product teams see project health, track decisions and brief themselves from sources they can check.

Role
Product Designer, end to end
Scope
IA, interactions, interface
Status
Interactive prototype

Information in fragments

Project information lives across task boards, design files, documents, discussions and status meetings. The home view brings portfolio health, launches and the projects needing attention into one place.

Keep both signals visible

A team-level update should not silently replace the overall assessment. An “On track” project and a “Blocked” design workstream stay visible together while the product manager reviews the impact.

AI that shows its sources

The AI brief cites evidence, shows uncertainty and respects permissions, with human review before anything is shared. Validation is proposed; no measured outcomes are claimed.

Showroom — Bay 03 / 03 · Practice concept

Snapchat

An independent case study on helping people find Stories, Spotlight and Lenses without slowing down Camera and Chat.

Role
UX & UI Designer
Scope
Research to final screens
Status
Not built or launched

Easy to get around, easy to miss

10 of 14 people said finding their way around was easy. Yet 12 of 14 rarely watch Spotlight or did not know it existed, and 8 of 15 find new features only by noticing them.

Give Explore one clear job

Stories, Spotlight and Lenses sit under one labelled Explore tab. Friends’ picks come first, and every pick has a send button. Camera and Chat stay exactly where they are.

Honest about the evidence

The research is a 15-person survey and 3 interviews, so a guide and not proof. Nothing here is built or tested, and the study sets out how I would test it.
